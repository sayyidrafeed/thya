Meet Thya, Your AI Business Assistant
Thya is an AI business agent designed to empower micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) with low digital adoption to simplify business management, organize records, and make data-informed decisions through accessible AI assistance.
Through natural-language interaction, Thya enables business owners to organize their business records, understand sales and expenses, analyze business performance, and generate actionable insights without requiring advanced digital or data-analysis skills. Beyond answering questions, Thya aims to assist with practical business tasks, helping owners move from simply recording information to understanding what it means and determining what to do next.

Why Does This Matter?
For culinary MSMEs, the challenge is not simply collecting business data, but making sense of it while keeping daily operations running. When business information is difficult to consolidate and analyze, owners may struggle to identify what is actually happening in their business. High sales figures, for instance, do not automatically indicate healthy profit margins, while frequent stock shortages or excess inventory may be difficult to anticipate without a clear understanding of sales patterns. These are potential challenges that need to be validated, rather than assumed to affect every culinary MSME.
The central issue, therefore, is the gap between having business data and being able to turn it into actionable insights. Understanding this gap requires examining how culinary MSME owners currently manage their data, which tasks require the most time and effort, and how they use the information to make operational decisions.
Situation
Culinary MSMEs generate data from daily sales, purchases, expenses, inventory, and promotional activities to support business operations.
Complication
This information may be scattered across different tools or manual records, making it time-consuming to consolidate, analyze, and translate into useful business insights.
Question
How can culinary MSME owners better understand their business performance and make operational decisions without spending excessive time and effort analyzing their data?

What Is Happening to MSMEs in Indonesia?
Micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs) are a major pillar of Indonesia’s economy. According to data compiled by the OECD, Indonesia had approximately 56.14 million MSMEs in 2024, accounting for 99.9% of the total business population and employing around 121.8 million workers [1]. Their sustainability therefore affects not only business owners, but also employees, suppliers, and the communities that depend on them.
One particularly relevant sector is food and beverage. According to Statistics Indonesia (BPS), the country had approximately 5.28 million food and beverage service businesses in 2024, an increase of 8.71% from the previous year [2]. Behind these numbers are thousands of daily business operations that require owners to balance customer satisfaction, product quality, sales, and operating costs.
Chapter 1: Problem Statement
Running a Business Requires More Than Generating Sales
Running a café or restaurant involves daily decisions about inventory, pricing, expenses, and promotions. These decisions are interconnected: rising ingredient costs can reduce profit margins, while promotions may increase sales without necessarily improving profitability. Changing consumer purchasing power can further complicate these decisions [3]. Making informed decisions requires more than experience or intuition. Owners need reliable information about their business, yet the data they generate through sales, purchases, expenses, and inventory may be scattered across spreadsheets, and any manual records. For example, knowing daily revenue does not necessarily reveal whether the business is profitable. Owners still need to combine sales figures, ingredient costs, and operating expenses to understand their actual performance.
A Bank Indonesia study involving 10,142 ultra-micro, micro, and small businesses found that digital adoption is influenced by factors such as business characteristics, owner capabilities, and infrastructure availability [4]. While the study does not directly measure the time spent analyzing business data, it highlights that adopting digital tools alone does not guarantee that information can be used effectively. The key question is how much time and effort business owners spend turning operational data into meaningful insights and decisions.
